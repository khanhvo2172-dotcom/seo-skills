# TrueProfit editorial standard

Condensed from the content manager's SEO writing playbook and SOP checklist (October 28, 2026). Skip a check that doesn't apply; don't add work just to complete a template.

## What good content does

- Answers the main query early, then explains the reasoning, trade-offs and next steps.
- Is accurate before it is polished. Never invent statistics, product capabilities, pricing, benchmarks, quotes, source claims or consensus.
- Connects advice to real merchant decisions: costs, margins, conversion, acquisition, fulfillment, retention and operational limits.
- Makes every section earn its place. No padding, repeated explanations or generic advice that fits any business.

## Reader and voice

- Default reader: a Shopify merchant or ecommerce operator. They may be new, but don't oversimplify; explain unfamiliar terms clearly and keep the nuance.
- Voice: conversational-professional, knowledgeable, practical and credible, like an experienced operator explaining what works and why.
- "I recommend" is fine occasionally when it adds judgment; never as a template, and never invent first-person experience.
- Use "you" and "we" naturally, not in every paragraph. Transitions carry the reasoning; phrasal verbs and idioms are fine in moderation.
- Most paragraphs run 2–4 lines. Don't split ideas into choppy one-sentence fragments to look scannable.

### Avoid

- Generic AI openings, empty claims, repetitive conclusions, formulaic transitions.
- Keyword stuffing and unnatural exact-match phrases.
- Corporate, academic, overly casual or chatbot wording.
- The same paragraph opener repeated across the article ("Most merchants", "If…", "Once…", "Here's the thing").
- Em dashes (none at all), excessive colon-led headings, and titles built as keyword plus a generic descriptor.
- Bullets that are only a short command and a period. Bullets help scanning; they don't replace analysis.
- Over-explaining basics, or repeating the same profitability warning in every section.
- Generic claims like "this is crucial for success" unless the next sentence explains exactly why.

## Research and sources

- Define the intent: learn, compare, choose a tool, solve a problem or act. Match the format (list, tutorial, comparison, review, benchmark, checklist, definition).
- Don't let a promotional goal override intent. A "best dropshipping apps" article prioritizes dropshipping apps, not TrueProfit.
- Use the SERP for expectations and an area to win (missing decision criterion, unclear explanation, stale detail, weak comparison, unsupported claim, missing practical guidance). Don't copy competitors' structure or claims.
- Prefer primary sources for features, pricing, policies, platform changes, technical definitions and official statistics. Official sources count as evidence alongside the ranking URLs.
- Tool pricing and plan limits: the provider's official pricing page. App availability and integrations: official product pages or app listings.
- Statistics: record the original source, publication date, geography, sample and definition when available, and make sure the number supports the exact claim. Don't turn an estimate, survey result, single example or category benchmark into a universal fact.
- Clean source links: remove UTM and similar tracking parameters when a clean canonical URL works. Keep TrueProfit's own campaign parameters on CTA and App Store links.
- If a claim can't be verified: qualify it, replace it with a better-supported statement, or remove it. `[Inference]`, `[Speculation]` and `[Unverified]` are draft markers only for claims that genuinely can't be verified; stop and ask about a material one.

## Outline and sections

- Organize around the reader's decision or task, not a keyword list. H2 for major questions or stages, H3 for meaningful subtopics. No headings just to fit a keyword.
- Each heading has a distinct job. If two would get nearly the same answer, they should be merged or sharpened (flag it if the outline is locked).
- Tables for genuine comparisons and compact reference data; bullets for short lists, steps or criteria; prose for reasoning and nuance.
- A strong section goes from a clear point to the explanation to what it means for the merchant.

## App and product roundups

- Choose apps by the keyword's intent and the reader's needs.
- Per app: short positioning (what it does, why it's in this list); Key Features relevant to the intent with their practical value; Pricing in one or two verified sentences (plan, billing basis, trial limits); Best For; Customer Sentiment only when reliable review patterns add insight. Never invent consensus or cherry-pick anecdotes.
- Keep entries comparable but not mechanically identical. Don't pad a weak entry or give one app disproportionate space without reason.
- Place TrueProfit where profit tracking complements the other tools (sourcing, importing, fulfillment, marketing). Never first by default, never as a substitute for a category it doesn't replace.

## TrueProfit mentions

- Positioning: real-time net profit analytics for Shopify merchants: profitability after all costs, product and ad performance, decisions based on clearer business results.
- Mention it only where its capabilities solve a problem the article discusses, and explain the specific connection.
- Verify current features, integrations and plan details before stating them.
- The reader's problem stays at the center. No landing-page tone; use a subtle, context-specific CTA.

## On-page SEO

- Primary keyword naturally in the title, introduction and relevant headings; not forced into every section. Related terms only where they clarify.
- Title communicates the article's actual value and matches intent.
- Meta description summarizes the real benefit in natural language; no keyword cramming, unsupported superlatives or promises the article doesn't keep.
- Internal links to genuinely useful pages with descriptive anchors; don't repeat the same exact-match anchor excessively. Check every link and destination.
- Headings, tables, lists, images and FAQs exist to help the reader, not only for SEO.

## Closing sections

- **Quick Recap:** 4–5 distinct takeaways in complete natural sentences; none restates the introduction or simply repeats a heading.
- **FAQs:** answer directly and briefly, then add the one clarification a reader needs. Genuine secondary questions only; don't copy body paragraphs.
- **Final Thoughts:** a practical conclusion or decision principle. No generic wrap-up ("In today's competitive landscape…") and no replay of the whole article.

## Submit checklist

- **Intent:** the opening answers the main query; the article completes the search task.
- **Accuracy:** numbers, dates, claims, pricing, features, integrations, formulas and links verified.
- **Logic:** sections flow, don't contradict each other, don't repeat the same point.
- **Depth:** each major section gives reasoning or useful detail, not surface description.
- **Original value:** analysis, a practical example, a trade-off or a clearer explanation beyond what competing pages repeat.
- **Voice:** no generic AI phrasing, repeated openers, awkward keyword insertions, excessive colon headings or em dashes.
- **SEO:** title, H2/H3 hierarchy, natural keyword use, meta description, internal links, clean source URLs.
- **TrueProfit:** relevant, accurate, understated, placed where it helps.
- **Finish:** FAQs add distinct value; Quick Recap has 4–5 distinct takeaways when required; Final Thoughts isn't a generic summary.

## Reusable writer brief

Write or update this TrueProfit SEO article for Shopify merchants and ecommerce operators. Put factual accuracy, search intent, practical value and clear reasoning before keyword optimization. Use a natural, conversational-professional, knowledgeable voice. Answer the main query early; explain why recommendations work, include concrete examples or calculations when useful, and note important trade-offs. Avoid generic AI phrasing, keyword stuffing, repetitive transitions, over-explaining, excessive bullets, em dashes and salesy TrueProfit mentions. Verify facts against primary sources and never invent statistics, pricing, features or consensus. Match the approved outline and give each heading a distinct purpose. When updating an existing article, keep accurate and useful copy and change only what genuinely needs improvement. Integrate TrueProfit only where it naturally helps the reader's specific task.
