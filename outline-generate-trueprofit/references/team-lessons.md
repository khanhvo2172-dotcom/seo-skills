# Team lessons for TrueProfit outlines

Distilled from the October 2026 outlines in the SEO sheet `Content Plan 10/2026` (Docs tabs named "insights & outline") and the reviewer comments on those outlines and their articles. Reviewers: the content lead and the SEO lead; each correction below came up in real reviews.

## 1. Intent and audience

- Stay inside the keyword's intent and its real audience. A "shopify business plan" article targets people about to open a store; a section on tracking the plan against live performance was cut as "too far from the intent".
- Mixed intent (e.g. "business plan" = planning document vs Shopify pricing plan): satisfy the dominant SERP intent, and cover the secondary one briefly.
- Don't go deeper than the topic needs (credit-card rate tables in a business-plan article were "not necessary").
- Broad topics: say "ecommerce" in the title and intro so the scope is clear (a "track BFCM performance" title was too generic without it).

## 2. Structure

- No overlapping headings. Real examples that were flagged: "How to Set Up Paid Search" vs "Step-by-Step Execution Strategy" (merge); "How to Analyze Performance After the Sale" duplicating the tracking phases; "Ad Spend and Budget Pacing" duplicating the marketing-metrics section.
- Put each point under the heading it belongs to: "measure social performance by profit" is not a social-media strategy, it belongs in the measurement section; profit metrics don't belong in a "sales & conversion metrics" table.
- A heading must deliver what it promises. A heading "Top-performing stores" makes readers (and search engines) expect benchmark numbers for top stores, not a note. "Eligibility requirements" means address/ID/plan requirements, not fees.
- Cut obvious or trivial sections and FAQs ("Should I use Facebook Ads for a new Shopify store?" — obviously yes; "Why you need a business plan" — too simple).
- Lists of metrics or comparisons work better as tables.
- Evergreen posts must not be framed to one period ("Q4 2026"). Update roundups: newest first, date in each H2 with the year (e.g. "Oct 9, 2026"), and focus on updates that affect merchants' profit (include agentic-commerce changes when relevant).

## 3. Benchmarks and data

- Lead with the numbers; readers came for the benchmark. Expert quotes and advice go after the data, and improvement advice goes in the "how to improve" H2.
- Give enough categories to be useful (a three-category AOV breakdown was "not useful").
- State the time range of every dataset.
- Prefer TrueProfit's own data when available; median is usually a better reference than average (explain the difference briefly).
- Credit the original source (data Shopify cites from Contentsquare is Contentsquare's data).
- Check for conflicts with numbers already published on trueprofit.io (e.g. the average Shopify conversion rate article) and flag them.

## 4. Metrics

- Pair ROAS with POAS (profit on ad spend); for a profit-analytics brand, POAS is the metric to emphasize.
- Paid search also needs impressions, impression share, CTR and clicks.
- Add ROAS/CTR benchmarks where a bidding or performance section discusses them, and link the ROAS benchmark page.
- Don't box merchants into email + paid ads; they also use SEO, organic social and partnerships.

## 5. Wording

- Use "Black Friday" rather than "BFCM" (more searched).
- Natural, merchant-focused headings; no keyword stuffing.

## 6. TrueProfit and sister products

- Mention TrueProfit only where it fits, normally the profitability/measurement section.
- Emphasize net profit (the core of the product) and that it tracks every cost a seller has: COGS, shipping, transaction fees, ad spend, refunds, apps and other expenses.
- Ad platforms and Shopify dashboards, even combined, don't show true profit because they miss the other running costs; that is why a profit-tracking tool is needed.
- Marketing Attribution: shows which orders an ad channel actually drove and whether they were profitable; Meta, Google Ads and Shopify cannot track this themselves, TrueProfit uses its own tracking. Its main message is real contribution, not just "compare campaign profitability". Reference: https://trueprofit.io/solutions/marketing-attribution
- Suggest a dashboard image beside the TrueProfit mention.
- Proveny (UGC/AI video ads) can be introduced in social-proof or UGC sections.

## 7. Practical advice

- Be concrete. Budget advice like "spend what you can afford, divide by 30.4" was too generic; give a test budget per product, then a scale budget.
- Shopify plan guidance by audience: Basic for beginners; Grow for newer sellers with high-ticket products who need more; Advanced/Plus for experienced sellers or those migrating with an existing customer base.
- Cost lists must be complete (e.g. platform alternatives: hosting, extensions, maintenance, apps, payment fees, domain, themes).

## 8. Example outline in team format

From "shopify competitor analysis" (Information intent), reviewed and kept lean:

```
H1. Shopify Competitor Analysis: How to Research Competitors and Find Opportunities to Grow
H2. How to Conduct a Shopify Competitor Analysis
H3. Identify Direct and Indirect Competitors
Coverage: Find businesses serving the same customer need, including relevant stores on other ecommerce platforms.
H3. Compare Products, Positioning, and Prices
Coverage: Compare equivalent products, benefits, bundles, discounts, and delivered prices using the same criteria.
H3. Review Marketing Channels, Ads, and Offers
Coverage: Examine search visibility, public ads, landing pages, newsletters, and recurring offers.
H3. Evaluate the Shopping Experience and Customer Feedback
Coverage: Review mobile navigation, product pages, checkout, delivery and returns information, and recurring customer complaints.
H3. Summarize Strengths, Weaknesses, and Market Gaps
Coverage: Separate documented observations from hypotheses and identify gaps worth testing.
H2. Best Tools for Shopify Competitor Analysis
Coverage: Use a concise comparison table showing each tool's task, available data, limitations, and free or paid access.
H3. What Competitor Research Tools Cannot Tell You
Coverage: Distinguish estimated traffic and detectable apps from verified revenue, conversion rates, inventory, or profit.
H2. How to Turn Competitor Insights Into Profitable Growth (area to win)
Coverage: Work through one finding, proposed test, cost calculation, and success criterion using the merchant's own economics.
H2. Final Thoughts
Coverage: End with the next research-and-test action without repeating the workflow.
H2. FAQs
H3. How often should I update my Shopify competitor analysis?
Answer coverage: Revisit before important product, pricing, or market decisions; avoid one universal interval.
H3. Do I need to buy from competitors to analyze them?
Answer coverage: Public research establishes a baseline; an optional purchase reveals delivery, packaging, and service details.
```

Listicle variant ("christmas marketing ideas"): a first H2 comparing ideas by budget, effort and time (area to win), then one numbered H2 per idea (`H2. 1. Launch a Christmas Countdown or Advent Calendar Campaign`) with one Coverage line each, a measurement H2 (area to win), two FAQs.
