Prefer the applicable specialist tool over forcing the general calculator into every outline. An article's passing mention of POD or dropshipping does not automatically change its theme; use a specialist tool only for relevant coverage. Do not add all calculators because a heading mentions profit.

Calculator results depend on the supplied inputs. Check whether packaging, fees, refunds, overhead and other relevant costs are included; account separately for omitted costs rather than claim the calculator captures every expense. ROAS measures attributed ad revenue relative to ad spend, not net profit. Gross profit and net profit are different measures. Theme/app detection does not establish a competitor's revenue, conversion rate, full tech stack or profitability.

For an advertising measurement heading, ROAS and net-profit guides are useful default candidates if those metrics are explained; map CAC to its budget/acquisition-cost H3 when one exists. Use a benchmark URL for actual benchmark coverage, not as a second interchangeable definition link. Choose the definition, benchmark or comparison destination that fits the passage. Optional references should state the condition for using them.

Do not import numeric benchmarks from a page without checking its date, population and applicability. A general ROAS/CAC average is not automatically a Christmas, BFCM or store-specific target.

## Illustrative placements

- Competitor research tools: theme and app detectors, with their public-data limitations.
- Pre-stock product validation: a profit calculator for the proposed selling price and cost assumptions; it does not prove demand.
- Ready-to-gift bundles: a profit calculator to test offer economics, allowing for packaging and fulfillment.
- Advertising measurement: ROAS guide plus ROAS calculator, net-profit guide, and CAC where acquisition costs are discussed.

Keep these as contextual body suggestions. Further Reading is reserved for verified relevant pillar/sub-pillar pages missing from the body plan.
