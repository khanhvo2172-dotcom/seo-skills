# Natural captions that give readers context

Khanh rejected literal scene descriptions as sounding like alt text, then approved contextual captions and requested the same treatment for every image in another article.

Write the caption from the source page and the surrounding section. Help a reader understand the idea, offer or story represented by this specific example. One useful sentence is usually enough; add a second only when a date, adaptation or qualification matters. Avoid a fixed formula across every image, repeating the section heading, or inventing performance results.

A visible caption is useful only when it adds context. Omit one that merely repeats an adjacent instruction under a self-explanatory tool/dashboard screenshot; keep required attribution separate and preserve pending human suggestions.

## Approved examples

**John Lewis, The Bear and the Hare**

> John Lewis's 2013 The Bear and the Hare makes a thoughtful gift the reason two friends can share Christmas.

This gives the still its story context. The rejected visual-only version described a white hare riding a brown bear through autumn woodland.

**Hershey's Holiday Bells**

> Hershey's Holiday Bells turns its chocolates into the stars of a Christmas performance, using their familiar shape to connect the product with the season.

This explains the creative idea, rather than listing the red, green and silver chocolates in the image.

**Sainsbury's, A Christmas to Savour**

> Sainsbury's 2021 A Christmas to Savour invites viewers to imagine their own festive gathering, placing its food at the heart of the celebration.

This connects the gathering to the role of the advertised food, rather than only describing a dinner table.

## Applying the approach to other article images

- An app caption can explain what the shown feature helps the article's reader do. Do not assert capabilities absent from the page or invent a dashboard result.
- A product caption can explain why the item illustrates the section's product category or gifting idea. Do not invent price, availability, material or benefits from appearance.
- A campaign caption can explain the audience, interaction, offer or product role. Distinguish observed creative choices from measured results.

Useful patterns from the marketing article:

> TANIT's Black Friday email invites subscribers to join a VIP list for early access, a format that can also be adapted for a Christmas campaign.

> Bombas's December 2022 email puts Christmas delivery at the center of the offer, giving shoppers a timely reason to complete their gift purchases.

> The Bearded Plantaholic frames its January discount as a thank-you to customers, offering a reason to return after the festive season.

The last example illustrates a January follow-up incentive. It does not establish that the offer was restricted to people who bought Christmas gifts.

## Caption, source and alt text have different jobs

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Visible description | Give readers natural campaign/product context and relevance. |
| Linked Source credit | Attribute and link to the actual source page. Keep the link separate from unlinked prose. |
| Alt suggestion | Concisely describe the image's relevant visible content for accessibility. |

Keep alt suggestions in the task manifest/source notes. The current Docs API helper cannot set image alt metadata; do not claim it did. CMS image triggers are a separate requested workflow, not an automatic part of adding captions.
