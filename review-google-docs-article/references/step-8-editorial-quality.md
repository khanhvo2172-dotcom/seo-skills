# Step 8: Editorial quality pass

Report-only. Applies the content manager's writing playbook and SOP checklist (October 28, 2026) to the selected article tab. Run it after Steps 7 and 6 so structure and facts are already known; don't repeat their findings here.

Ownership: when Step 6 runs, it owns verifying facts, prices and statistics, and Step 8 check 2 only covers how sources are presented (date, scope, universal-fact overreach) and wording that overstates. When Step 8 runs alone, check 2 includes spot-verifying the most decision-relevant prices and statistics on official sources.

Report only what needs a decision or an edit, using the main reporting rule. A check that doesn't apply to this article is skipped, not reported.

## Checks

1. **Intent.** The introduction answers the main query early, and the article as a whole completes the searcher's task. The format (list, tutorial, comparison, review, benchmark, checklist, definition) matches the intent.
2. **Accuracy beyond marked sentences.** Pricing, plan limits and app features are checked on the provider's official pricing or product page or app listing. Statistics carry their original source, date and, where relevant, geography, sample and definition, and support the exact claim. Estimates, single examples and category benchmarks aren't presented as universal facts. Every calculation is correct and its assumptions are labelled.
3. **Logic.** Sections flow in the reader's working order, don't contradict each other and don't repeat the same point. The same profitability warning isn't restated in every section.
4. **Depth.** Each major section moves from a clear point to the reasoning to what it means for the merchant, with trade-offs or limits where a recommendation could fail. Flag surface-level description and generic claims ("this is crucial for success") that aren't explained.
5. **Voice.** Flag em dashes (none are allowed anywhere: body, tables, captions, image alt text, meta description), generic AI openings and phrasing, the same paragraph opener starting three or more paragraphs ("Most merchants", "If…", "Once…", "Here's the thing"; deliberate repeated labels inside parallel list entries, such as "What to watch:", don't count), "I recommend" used as a template, awkward exact-match keyword insertions, excessive colon-led headings, choppy one-sentence paragraphs, and bullets that are only a command with a period.
6. **SEO.** The title states the article's real value without a keyword-plus-generic-descriptor or keyword-colon formula. H2/H3 hierarchy is logical. Keyword use is natural. The meta description (max 155 characters) summarizes the real promise without unsupported superlatives. Internal anchors are descriptive and the same exact-match anchor isn't repeated excessively. External source links are clean: recommend removing `utm_*` and similar tracking parameters from third-party source URLs when a clean canonical URL works. Never touch TrueProfit's own campaign parameters on CTA or App Store links (Step 5 still handles AI-tool `utm_source` removal).
7. **TrueProfit fit.** Each mention solves a problem already set up in that section, describes capabilities accurately and stays understated. In roundups, TrueProfit isn't first by default and isn't framed as a substitute for a category it doesn't replace.
8. **App roundups.** Each app has short positioning, Key Features relevant to the intent, Pricing in one or two verified sentences, Best For, and Customer Sentiment only when backed by reliable review patterns. Entries are comparable without being mechanically identical, and no app gets disproportionate space without reason.
9. **Finish.** Quick Recap, when present, has 4–5 distinct takeaways that don't restate the introduction or headings. In a list article a bullet may name items, as long as it adds the reason or decision rather than repeating the heading list. FAQ answers are direct and brief with one clarification, not copied body paragraphs (Step 4 covers redundancy). Final Thoughts gives a practical conclusion or decision principle, not a generic summary.

## Stop and ask Khanh

Raise these at the top of the report instead of proposing a fix:

- A material claim has no reliable source and cannot be safely qualified.
- Official sources disagree, or pricing or feature availability is unclear.
- The article conflicts with search intent, or another trueprofit.io article targets the same main keyword or primary intent (check titles and slugs in `https://trueprofit.io/blog.xml`; overlap on one section only needs a cross-link, reported as a normal item).
- A claim overstates TrueProfit or presents it as a substitute for an unrelated tool.

## Output

Add actionable rows to the main findings (location, exact text, why it matters, concrete correction); when Step 8 runs alone, use the same format as the whole report. This is pre-publish QA, not an audit, so the handoff note doesn't list unchanged sections. Propose the smallest edit that solves each problem; don't suggest rewriting accurate, useful copy just to make it sound different.
